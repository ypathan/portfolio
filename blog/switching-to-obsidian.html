Switching to Obsidian

As I have talked about before, I have switched from Notion to Obsidian due to Notion having some issues. Previously I talked about the technical side, how I have tried different workflows and different deployments. This is just some thoughts on the whole process and journey and how I feel about it.

Advantages Obsidian gives me that Notion did not provide:

  • Local storage: my notes are in my control. I have them on my laptop, synced to my MacBook, version controlled using Git to GitHub, and uploaded to my VPS so that I can share them publicly, specifically my worklogs and work-related documents that I share with colleagues.
  • Access to AI: Notion AI was an extra payment of around $20 or something. Now I can just use opencode, it provides free models that I can use for simple tasks, and I can connect my DeepSeek API when I need something heavier.
  • I had to create my own note-sharing workflow apart from Notion: this taught me about caching (validation, what type of files to cache), using Docker mount points, and nginx performance tuning (workers, threads). Although I was asking AI to do it, at least now I have a rough idea of how these tools work, and if I need them in the future, that idea in my head with some help from AI should be good enough to point me in the right direction.
  • I now have something I use on a daily basis hosted on my own server after so long: from 2021 when I got introduced to Linux and self-hosting, for 90% of the time during these 5 years I've had my own server to play around with stuff, but for the first time now I have had a real-world use case for it and a dependency on it on a day-to-day basis.
