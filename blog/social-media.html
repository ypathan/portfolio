Social Media

I have written about this matter before, and I have thought about this extensively. In fact, one of my first writings was on this topic, on the bad effects social media has on the brain, the dopaminemaxing, the time wastage, and the mind-control mechanism to zombify the population.

But today I was thinking about this from another angle: the angle of the algorithm. The algorithm knows a lot about us, more than friends and family do. It knows us so deep that no one knows us this deep: our preferences, our views, our likes and dislikes, our opinions, the kind of music we like, our interests, etc. Every little detail. It knows what we're thinking about on a particular day (because we would be searching that). It knows how we're feeling (because of the content we consume in those moments), and it knows what is happening in our lives, because in those moments we would be searching that.

On one end there are benefits to this algorithm. This algorithm is the reason the internet is so useful; otherwise it would just be a dump collection of websites. The Google search algorithm, the YouTube content algorithm, and the ones from Meta, Amazon and other big giants are what make the internet so useful.

But on the other end, they know everything about you. They have the ability to silently re-educate you. They have the ability, if one day they wish to crack down on a portion of society that holds a particular view, to know exactly who to target, because all the corporations run at the mercy of the government. As they say, no one is above the law, and the law is interpreted by those in the government and bureaucracy. If they wish, invading Iraq is a necessity, and if they wish, Russia invading Ukraine is a war crime. So what is there to say that one day they won't use social media to crack down on society and filter out specific views?

With all the shadow banning happening around Kashmir and Palestine, this shouldn't be an alien concept to us.

Think about it. We form opinions based on what we see and what we experience. Nowadays very few actually see things first hand; most just see what they are shown through their screens, and what you see through your screen can be tailored. For example, if there is a hardline fundamentalist of a specific philosophy, the most extreme version is to put them in re-education camps and, through hard labour and torture, force them into believing, not believing, or "not acting" upon their ideology. That is the most extreme version. But sometimes you don't have to go so far.

If a person uses social media and actively views content through it, which more than half the population does, you just have to slowly show them content that is what you want them to believe. After viewing adequate content on that view over a long period of time, they would automatically start believing it, if not giving thought to this alternative idea. I believe this is how Facebook was used to change or influence voting at some point. So what is there to say that we are not being fed propaganda content on a daily basis to soften our views on masculinity, child-bearing, governments, economy and ownership?

Because psychologically, what makes us so vulnerable to this influence is that usually when we use social media, we are not at our desks; we are not in postures where we are thinking critically or where our brain waves are at the levels where people think critically. Our brain waves usually go to a level where we take a backseat and allow what's coming our way to come at us. Social media companies make it so that we don't have to put thought into using these products. We just have to open the app; we don't have to search, because search requires brain power. Upon opening, a video starts to play immediately and we sit back and relax. When the video finishes, we don't have to put in any more thought to select the next video, the system will automatically choose one for us, it's just a simple swipe away.

And so at this level our brain is not thinking; it is just allowing everything through. The firewall is down. Because of that, things come in and they continue coming in, time passes like a bullet train, ideas float around us and we don't even notice them, and these ideas carve out a space for themselves in our subconscious mind, hence "re-education without the effort."
